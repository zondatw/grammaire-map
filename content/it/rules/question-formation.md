## Explanation

Italian questions are simpler to form than in many languages — there is no auxiliary "do/does" needed.

**Yes/no questions:** Use the same word order as a statement, simply adding a rising intonation (in speech) or a question mark (in writing). The subject pronoun can be moved to the end for clarity:

- *Parli italiano?* (Do you speak Italian?)
- *Maria lavora domani?* / *Lavora domani Maria?*

**Question words:**

| Italian | English | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| chi | who | *Chi è quella persona?* |
| che / cosa / che cosa | what | *Cosa fai?* / *Che cosa mangi?* |
| quando | when | *Quando parti?* |
| dove | where | *Dove abiti?* |
| come | how | *Come stai?* |
| perché | why | *Perché sei triste?* |
| quanto/a | how much | *Quanto costa?* |
| quanti/e | how many | *Quanti anni hai?* |
| quale/i | which | *Quale preferisci?* |

**Come** and **dove** drop their final -e before *è*: *Com'è?* (How is it?), *Dov'è?* (Where is it?)

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| Parli inglese? | Do you speak English? |
| Dov'è la stazione? | Where is the station? |
| Quanti anni hai? | How old are you? |
| Perché non vieni? | Why aren't you coming? |
| Con chi parli? | Who are you talking to? |

## Pitfall

Italian does not invert subject and verb the way English does in questions (*Are you...?* / *Do you...?*). Just use a question mark and/or rising intonation — word order changes are optional and stylistic, not required.
