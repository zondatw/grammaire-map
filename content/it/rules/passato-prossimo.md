## Explanation

The **passato prossimo** is the most common past tense in spoken Italian, used for completed past actions. It is a compound tense formed with **avere** or **essere** + the **past participle**.

**Forming the past participle:**
- -are verbs → **-ato**: *parlare* → *parlato*
- -ere verbs → **-uto**: *vendere* → *venduto*
- -ire verbs → **-ito**: *dormire* → *dormito*

**Common irregular past participles:**

| Infinitive | Past Participle |
|-----------|-----------------|
| essere | stato |
| fare | fatto |
| dire | detto |
| scrivere | scritto |
| leggere | letto |
| vedere | visto |
| prendere | preso |
| aprire | aperto |
| venire | venuto |
| mettere | messo |
| chiedere | chiesto |

**With avere:** The participle does **not** change form (unless preceded by a direct object pronoun).
- *Ho mangiato la pizza.* (I ate the pizza.)
- *Abbiamo visto un film.* (We saw a film.)

**With essere:** The participle agrees with the subject in gender and number.
- *Sono andato* (m.) / *Sono andata* (f.) — I went
- *Siamo andati* (m.pl.) / *Siamo andate* (f.pl.) — We went

Verbs that take **essere**: most intransitive motion/state verbs (see rule *essere-vs-avere* for the full list), all reflexive verbs.

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| Ho mangiato una mela. | I ate an apple. |
| Hai visto quel film? | Did you see that film? |
| È andata a Roma. | She went to Rome. |
| Siamo partiti ieri. | We left yesterday. |
| Non ho dormito bene. | I didn't sleep well. |

## Pitfall

Do not use avere for all verbs automatically. Verbs of motion and state (andare, venire, partire, arrivare, uscire, etc.) require **essere**, and the participle must agree with the subject. *Ho andato* is incorrect — it must be *Sono andato/a*.
