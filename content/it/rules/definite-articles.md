## Explanation

Italian has six definite articles. The choice depends on the noun's gender, number, and the first letter(s) of the following word.

- **il** — masculine singular before most consonants: *il libro* (the book)
- **lo** — masculine singular before s+consonant, z, gn, ps, x, y: *lo studente* (the student), *lo zaino* (the backpack)
- **la** — feminine singular before consonants: *la casa* (the house)
- **l'** — masculine or feminine singular before a vowel: *l'amico* (the friend, m.), *l'amica* (the friend, f.)
- **i** — masculine plural (regular consonants): *i libri* (the books)
- **gli** — masculine plural before vowels, s+consonant, z, gn: *gli studenti* (the students), *gli amici* (the friends)
- **le** — feminine plural: *le case* (the houses)

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| il libro | the book |
| lo studente | the student |
| la casa | the house |
| l'amico | the friend (m.) |
| i libri | the books |
| gli zaini | the backpacks |
| le ragazze | the girls |

## Pitfall

The choice between **il** and **lo** is determined by the first letter(s) of the *next* word, not by the noun's gender alone. So it is *il grande libro* but *lo stesso libro* — the article changes depending on what immediately follows it.
