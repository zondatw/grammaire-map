## Explanation

**Essere** (to be) and **avere** (to have) are the two most important Italian verbs. Both are highly irregular and also serve as auxiliary verbs in compound tenses.

**Essere** — present tense:

| Person | Form |
|--------|------|
| io | sono |
| tu | sei |
| lui/lei | è |
| noi | siamo |
| voi | siete |
| loro | sono |

**Avere** — present tense:

| Person | Form |
|--------|------|
| io | ho |
| tu | hai |
| lui/lei | ha |
| noi | abbiamo |
| voi | avete |
| loro | hanno |

Note: *ho*, *hai*, *ha*, *hanno* are spelled with **h** (not pronounced) to distinguish them from *o* (or), *ai* (to the), *a* (to), *anno* (year).

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| Sono italiano. | I am Italian. |
| Sei stanco? | Are you tired? |
| Lei è medica. | She is a doctor. |
| Ho fame. | I am hungry. (lit. I have hunger.) |
| Hai freddo? | Are you cold? (lit. Do you have cold?) |
| Hanno ragione. | They are right. (lit. They have reason.) |

## Pitfall

Many physical states use **avere** in Italian where English uses "to be": *avere fame* (to be hungry), *avere sete* (to be thirsty), *avere caldo* (to be hot), *avere freddo* (to be cold), *avere paura* (to be afraid), *avere sonno* (to be sleepy).
