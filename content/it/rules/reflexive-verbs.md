## Explanation

Reflexive verbs express an action done to or for oneself. They are identified by the **-si** ending on the infinitive and use reflexive pronouns.

**Reflexive pronouns:**

| Person | Pronoun |
|--------|---------|
| io | mi |
| tu | ti |
| lui/lei | si |
| noi | ci |
| voi | vi |
| loro | si |

**Position of reflexive pronouns:**
- Before a conjugated verb: *Mi sveglio alle sette.* (I wake up at seven.)
- Attached to an infinitive (drop the final -e): *Voglio alzarmi tardi.* / *Voglio mi alzare* ✗ — attach to infinitive.
- With modal verbs, the pronoun can precede the modal or attach to the infinitive: *Mi voglio alzare* = *Voglio alzarmi.*

**Common reflexive verbs:**

| Verb | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| alzarsi | to get up |
| chiamarsi | to be called / named |
| lavarsi | to wash oneself |
| svegliarsi | to wake up |
| vestirsi | to get dressed |
| sedersi | to sit down |
| sentirsi | to feel |
| fermarsi | to stop |
| incontrarsi | to meet each other |
| divertirsi | to enjoy oneself |

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| Mi chiamo Marco. | My name is Marco. |
| Ti svegli presto? | Do you wake up early? |
| Si alza alle sei. | He/she gets up at six. |
| Ci laviamo le mani. | We wash our hands. |
| Vi divertite alla festa? | Are you enjoying yourselves at the party? |
| Si vestono in fretta. | They get dressed quickly. |

## Pitfall

Reflexive verbs in the passato prossimo always use **essere** as the auxiliary, and the past participle must agree with the subject: *Mi sono alzato* (m.) / *Mi sono alzata* (f.).
