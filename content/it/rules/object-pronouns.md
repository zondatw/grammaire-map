## Explanation

Italian has both **direct object pronouns** (answering "who/what?") and **indirect object pronouns** (answering "to/for whom?").

**Direct object pronouns:**

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| me | mi | *Mi vede.* (He sees me.) |
| you (sing.) | ti | *Ti chiamo.* (I'll call you.) |
| him | lo | *Lo conosco.* (I know him.) |
| her/it (f.) | la | *La vedo.* (I see her/it.) |
| us | ci | *Ci aiuta.* (He helps us.) |
| you (pl.) | vi | *Vi aspetto.* (I'm waiting for you all.) |
| them (m.) | li | *Li invito.* (I'm inviting them.) |
| them (f.) | le | *Le compro.* (I'm buying them.) |

**Indirect object pronouns:**

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| to me | mi | *Mi parla.* (He speaks to me.) |
| to you (sing.) | ti | *Ti scrivo.* (I write to you.) |
| to him | gli | *Gli telefono.* (I call him.) |
| to her | le | *Le rispondo.* (I answer her.) |
| to us | ci | *Ci manda un messaggio.* |
| to you (pl.) | vi | *Vi dico la verità.* |
| to them | gli | *Gli mando una lettera.* |

**Placement:** Before the conjugated verb, or attached to an infinitive (drop final -e).

**Combined pronouns** (indirect + direct): The indirect pronoun comes first and its form changes slightly:

| Indirect | + lo | + la | + li | + le | + ne |
|----------|------|------|------|------|------|
| mi → me | me lo | me la | me li | me le | me ne |
| ti → te | te lo | te la | te li | te le | te ne |
| gli/le → glie | glielo | gliela | glieli | gliele | gliene |
| ci → ce | ce lo | ce la | ce li | ce le | ce ne |
| vi → ve | ve lo | ve la | ve li | ve le | ve ne |

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| Lo vedo ogni giorno. | I see him every day. |
| Gli mando un messaggio. | I'm sending him a message. |
| Me lo dai? | Will you give it to me? |
| Non la conosco. | I don't know her. |
| Glielo dico domani. | I'll tell him/her it tomorrow. |

## Pitfall

**gli** vs. **le**: In modern Italian, *gli* is used as the indirect object for both masculine singular (*gli* = to him) and plural (*gli* = to them), as well as in combined forms (*glielo*). *Le* is only indirect "to her." Do not confuse with the definite article *le* or the direct object *le* (them, f.).
