## Explanation

Italian possessive adjectives agree in gender and number with the **thing possessed** (not the owner). They are almost always preceded by the definite article.

| Person | Masc. Sing. | Fem. Sing. | Masc. Pl. | Fem. Pl. |
|--------|-------------|------------|-----------|----------|
| my | mio | mia | miei | mie |
| your (tu) | tuo | tua | tuoi | tue |
| his/her/its | suo | sua | suoi | sue |
| our | nostro | nostra | nostri | nostre |
| your (voi) | vostro | vostra | vostri | vostre |
| their | loro | loro | loro | loro |

**loro** is invariable (never changes form).

**Important exception — singular unmodified family members:** The definite article is **omitted** with close singular family members when no adjective or modifier is present:
- *mia madre* (my mother) — no article
- *tuo fratello* (your brother) — no article
- *la mia cara madre* (my dear mother) — article required (adjective present)
- *i miei fratelli* (my brothers) — article required (plural)
- *la loro madre* (their mother) — article required (loro always takes article)

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| il mio libro | my book |
| la tua casa | your house |
| i suoi amici | his/her friends |
| la nostra famiglia | our family |
| mia sorella | my sister (no article — singular family member) |
| il loro padre | their father (loro always takes article) |

## Pitfall

**suo/sua/suoi/sue** means both "his," "her," and "its" — the form is determined by the noun's gender, not the owner's. *il suo libro* can mean "his book," "her book," or "its book" depending on context.
