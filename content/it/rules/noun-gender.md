## Explanation

Italian nouns have grammatical gender (masculine or feminine) and must be learned with their article. Endings provide useful clues, though exceptions exist.

- **-o** endings are usually **masculine** (plural **-i**): *libro/libri* (book/books), *ragazzo/ragazzi*
- **-a** endings are usually **feminine** (plural **-e**): *casa/case* (house/houses), *ragazza/ragazze*
- **-e** endings can be **either gender** (plural **-i**): *il fiore/i fiori* (flower), *la notte/le notti* (night)
- Nouns ending in **-zione**, **-tà**, **-tù** are always **feminine**: *la stazione*, *la città*, *la virtù*
- Some nouns are irregular: *il problema* (masculine despite -a), *la mano* (feminine despite -o)
- Nouns ending in a consonant are usually borrowed words and are masculine: *il bar*, *il film*

## Examples

| Italian | English | Gender |
|---------|---------|--------|
| il libro | the book | masculine |
| la casa | the house | feminine |
| il fiore | the flower | masculine |
| la notte | the night | feminine |
| il problema | the problem | masculine (exception) |
| la mano | the hand | feminine (exception) |
| la stazione | the station | feminine (-zione) |
| la città | the city | feminine (-tà) |

## Pitfall

Do not assume all nouns ending in **-a** are feminine — *il problema*, *il programma*, *il tema*, *il clima* are all masculine. Always learn a noun together with its article.
