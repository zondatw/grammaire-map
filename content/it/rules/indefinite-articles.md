## Explanation

Italian indefinite articles correspond to English "a/an." The form depends on the gender of the noun and the first letter(s) of the following word.

- **un** — masculine, before most consonants or vowels: *un libro* (a book), *un amico* (a friend)
- **uno** — masculine, before s+consonant, z, gn, ps, x, y: *uno studente* (a student), *uno zaino* (a backpack)
- **una** — feminine, before a consonant: *una casa* (a house), *una ragazza* (a girl)
- **un'** — feminine, before a vowel: *un'amica* (a friend, f.), *un'ora* (an hour)

There are **no plural indefinite articles** in Italian. To express "some," use the **partitive article** (*del*, *dello*, *della*, *dell'*, *dei*, *degli*, *delle*): *dei libri* (some books), *delle ragazze* (some girls).

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| un libro | a book |
| uno studente | a student |
| una casa | a house |
| un'amica | a (female) friend |
| dei libri | some books |
| degli studenti | some students |
| delle case | some houses |

## Pitfall

Do not use **un'** with masculine nouns — it is only for feminine nouns before a vowel. A masculine noun before a vowel takes plain **un**: *un errore* (NOT *un'errore*), *un uomo* (NOT *un'uomo*).
