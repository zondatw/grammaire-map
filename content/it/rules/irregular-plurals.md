## Explanation

Most Italian nouns form their plural by changing the final vowel, but several categories have irregular or spelling-adjusted plurals.

**Nouns ending in -ca / -ga** add -h- to preserve the hard sound:
- *amica* → *amiche* (female friends)
- *banca* → *banche* (banks)
- *collega* → *colleghi* (male) or *colleghe* (female)

**Nouns ending in -co / -go** — varies by stress:
- Stressed on second-to-last syllable → keep hard sound: *fuoco* → *fuochi*, *lago* → *laghi*
- Stressed earlier → often soften: *amico* → *amici*, *medico* → *medici*
- Exception: *greco* → *greci*

**Nouns ending in -io:**
- Unstressed -io → single -i: *figlio* → *figli*, *specchio* → *specchi*
- Stressed -ìo → -ìi: *zio* → *zii*

**Nouns that change gender in the plural** (a small group of body parts, originally neuter in Latin):
- *il braccio* → *le braccia* (arms)
- *il ginocchio* → *le ginocchia* (knees)
- *il dito* → *le dita* (fingers)
- *l'uovo* → *le uova* (eggs)
- *il labbro* → *le labbra* (lips)

**Invariable nouns** (no change in plural):
- Nouns ending in a stressed vowel: *la città* → *le città*, *il caffè* → *i caffè*
- Nouns ending in a consonant: *il bar* → *i bar*, *il film* → *i film*
- Shortened nouns: *la foto* → *le foto*, *la moto* → *le moto*

## Examples

| Singular | Plural | Note |
|----------|--------|------|
| l'amica | le amiche | -ca → -che |
| il fuoco | i fuochi | stressed -co → -chi |
| l'amico | gli amici | earlier stress → -ci |
| il figlio | i figli | unstressed -io → -i |
| il braccio | le braccia | changes gender |
| la città | le città | invariable |

## Pitfall

Body part nouns that change gender (*le braccia*, *le dita*, *le uova*) are feminine plural but come from masculine singulars. This often confuses learners: *Ho le braccia stanche* (My arms are tired) — feminine agreement required.
