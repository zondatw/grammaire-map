## Explanation

Italian adjectives must agree in **gender** and **number** with the noun they modify.

**4-form adjectives** (ending in -o): change to -o/-a/-i/-e

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | bello | belli |
| Feminine | bella | belle |

**2-form adjectives** (ending in -e): change to -e/-i

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | grande | grandi |
| Feminine | grande | grandi |

**Position:** Most adjectives follow the noun: *un libro interessante*, *una casa grande*. A set of common adjectives typically precede the noun:
- bello, brutto (beautiful, ugly)
- buono, cattivo (good, bad)
- grande, piccolo (big, small)
- nuovo, vecchio (new, old)
- giovane (young)
- lungo, breve (long, short)

When **bello** precedes a noun, it follows the pattern of the definite article: *bel libro*, *bello studente*, *bell'amico*, *bei libri*, *begli studenti*.

## Examples

| Italian | English |
|---------|---------|
| un ragazzo alto | a tall boy |
| una ragazza alta | a tall girl |
| ragazzi alti | tall boys |
| ragazze alte | tall girls |
| un grande attore | a great actor |
| una bella giornata | a beautiful day |
| libri interessanti | interesting books |

## Pitfall

When an adjective modifies two or more nouns of different genders, use the **masculine plural** form: *un ragazzo e una ragazza italiani*.
