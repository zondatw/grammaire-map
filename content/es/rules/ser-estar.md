## Explanation

Spanish has two verbs meaning "to be": **ser** and **estar**. Choosing the right one is one of the most important skills in Spanish. This rule introduces the basics; see *Ser vs. Estar (Deep Dive)* for the full comparison.

**Ser** — used for identity and essential, permanent characteristics:
- Identity and names: *Soy María.*
- Origin and nationality: *Soy de México. Somos españoles.*
- Profession: *Es médico.*
- Permanent traits: *El cielo es azul.*
- Time and dates: *Son las tres. Hoy es lunes.*

**Conjugation of ser (present):**
| yo | tú | él/ella | nosotros | vosotros | ellos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| soy | eres | es | somos | sois | son |

**Estar** — used for location and temporary states:
- Location: *Estoy en casa. Madrid está en España.*
- Temporary states and conditions: *Estoy cansado. Ella está enferma.*
- Emotions: *Estamos contentos.*

**Conjugation of estar (present):**
| yo | tú | él/ella | nosotros | vosotros | ellos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| estoy | estás | está | estamos | estáis | están |

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| Soy estudiante. | I am a student. |
| Somos de Argentina. | We are from Argentina. |
| Estoy en la biblioteca. | I am in the library. |
| Está cansada hoy. | She is tired today. |
| Son las dos de la tarde. | It is two in the afternoon. |
| Estamos muy contentos. | We are very happy. |

## Pitfall

A common mistake is using *ser* for location: **never** say *"Soy en casa"* — locations always use **estar**. Similarly, don't use *estar* for nationality or profession: say *"Es profesora"*, not *"Está profesora"*.
