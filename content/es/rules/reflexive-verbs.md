## Explanation

Reflexive verbs indicate that the subject performs an action on or for itself. They are identified in the dictionary by the suffix **-se** on the infinitive: *levantarse*, *llamarse*, *ducharse*.

**Reflexive pronouns:**
| Person | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| yo | me |
| tú | te |
| él/ella/usted | se |
| nosotros | nos |
| vosotros | os |
| ellos/ustedes | se |

**Placement rules:**
1. **Before a conjugated verb:** *Me levanto a las siete.* (I get up at seven.)
2. **Attached to an infinitive:** *Quiero levantarme temprano.* or *Me quiero levantar temprano.*
3. **Attached to a gerund (-ndo form):** *Estoy duchándome.* or *Me estoy duchando.*

**Common reflexive verbs:**
- *llamarse* — to be called/named
- *levantarse* — to get up
- *ducharse* — to shower
- *acostarse* — to go to bed
- *sentarse* — to sit down
- *vestirse* — to get dressed
- *despertarse* — to wake up

Some verbs change meaning when reflexive: *ir* (to go) → *irse* (to leave/go away); *dormir* (to sleep) → *dormirse* (to fall asleep).

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| Me llamo Carlos. | My name is Carlos. |
| ¿A qué hora te levantas? | What time do you get up? |
| Se ducha cada mañana. | He showers every morning. |
| Nos acostamos tarde. | We go to bed late. |
| Quiero ducharme ahora. | I want to shower now. |
| Estoy vistiéndome. | I'm getting dressed. |

## Pitfall

The reflexive pronoun must match the subject — don't use *se* for every person. Saying *"Me llamo"* and *"Te llamas"* are different people. Also, when attaching a pronoun to a gerund, a written accent is often required to preserve stress: *duchándo**me*** (not *duchandome*).
