## Explanation

Spanish regular verbs fall into three groups based on their infinitive ending: **-ar**, **-er**, and **-ir**. Remove the infinitive ending and add the appropriate personal endings.

**-ar verbs** (e.g., *hablar* — to speak):
| yo | tú | él/ella | nosotros | vosotros | ellos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hablo | hablas | habla | hablamos | habláis | hablan |

**-er verbs** (e.g., *comer* — to eat):
| yo | tú | él/ella | nosotros | vosotros | ellos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| como | comes | come | comemos | coméis | comen |

**-ir verbs** (e.g., *vivir* — to live):
| yo | tú | él/ella | nosotros | vosotros | ellos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vivo | vives | vive | vivimos | vivís | viven |

**Uses of the present tense:**
- Current actions: *Estudio español.* (I study / am studying Spanish.)
- Habitual actions: *Como a las dos.* (I eat at two.)
- General truths: *El sol sale por el este.* (The sun rises in the east.)
- Near future (with time expression): *Mañana trabajo.* (I work tomorrow.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| Hablo español. | I speak Spanish. |
| ¿Comes pizza? | Do you eat pizza? |
| Vivimos en Madrid. | We live in Madrid. |
| Ella trabaja mucho. | She works a lot. |
| Ellos beben agua. | They drink water. |
| ¿Escribís cartas? | Do you (pl.) write letters? |

## Pitfall

The Spanish present tense covers both "I speak" and "I am speaking" — there is no separate present continuous required for simple statements. Also, subject pronouns (*yo*, *tú*, etc.) are usually omitted because the verb ending already indicates the person.
