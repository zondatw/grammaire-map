## Explanation

In Spanish, adjectives must agree with the noun they modify in both **gender** (masculine/feminine) and **number** (singular/plural).

**Standard -o/-a adjectives:**
| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | alto | altos |
| Feminine | alta | altas |

**Adjectives ending in -e or a consonant** — same form for masculine and feminine, add -s or -es for plural:
- *inteligente → inteligentes* (both genders)
- *azul → azules*, *fácil → fáciles*

**Position:** Most adjectives follow the noun in Spanish (unlike English):
- *un coche rojo* (a red car), *una casa grande* (a big house)

Some adjectives **precede** the noun, especially those expressing quantity, order, or subjective evaluation: *mucho*, *poco*, *primer*, *buen*, *mal*, *gran*.

**Shortening (apocope):** Several adjectives drop their final -o before a masculine singular noun:
- *bueno → buen*: *un buen libro*
- *malo → mal*: *un mal día*
- *grande → gran* (before any singular noun): *un gran hombre*, *una gran mujer*

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| un chico alto | a tall boy |
| una chica alta | a tall girl |
| unos chicos altos | some tall boys |
| unas chicas altas | some tall girls |
| un buen libro | a good book |
| una buena idea | a good idea |
| un gran artista | a great artist |
| una gran ciudad | a great city |

## Pitfall

Don't forget that *grande* shortens to *gran* before **any** singular noun (masculine or feminine), and it changes meaning: after the noun *grande* means "large/big," but before the noun *gran* means "great/impressive."
