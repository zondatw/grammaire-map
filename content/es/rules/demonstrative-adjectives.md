## Explanation

Spanish demonstrative adjectives point to the relative distance of a noun from the speaker. There are three levels of distance (unlike English, which has only two: this/that).

| Distance | Masculine Sg | Feminine Sg | Masculine Pl | Feminine Pl | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Near (speaker) | este | esta | estos | estas | this / these |
| Mid (listener) | ese | esa | esos | esas | that / those |
| Far (from both) | aquel | aquella | aquellos | aquellas | that over there |

**Placement:** Demonstrative adjectives go **before** the noun they modify.

**Neuter demonstratives** (refer to an idea or unidentified thing, not a specific noun):
- *esto* (this), *eso* (that), *aquello* (that over there)
- *¿Qué es esto?* (What is this?)

**Note on accents:** Modern RAE (Real Academia Española) guidelines no longer require accent marks on demonstrative pronouns (*éste*, *ése*, *aquél*), though you may still see them in older texts.

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| este libro | this book |
| esta silla | this chair |
| estos zapatos | these shoes |
| esas flores | those flowers |
| ese hombre | that man |
| aquel edificio | that building over there |
| aquellas montañas | those mountains (in the distance) |
| ¿Qué es eso? | What is that? |

## Pitfall

The three-way distinction (*este / ese / aquel*) is uniquely Spanish. English speakers default to just "this" and "that." Use *ese/esa* when something is near the listener but not the speaker, and *aquel/aquella* for something clearly far from both.
