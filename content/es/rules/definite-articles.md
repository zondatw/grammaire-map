## Explanation

Spanish has four definite articles that correspond to English "the." They agree in **gender** (masculine/feminine) and **number** (singular/plural) with the noun they modify.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | el | los |
| Feminine | la | las |

**Contractions:** Spanish has two mandatory contractions:
- **de + el → del** (never "de el")
- **a + el → al** (never "a el")

Note: These contractions do NOT apply to the pronoun "él" (he): *Hablo de él* (I talk about him).

**Special case:** Use **el** (not la) before feminine singular nouns beginning with a stressed **a-** or **ha-** sound: *el agua*, *el hacha*, *el águila*. In the plural, revert to **las**: *las aguas*.

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| el libro | the book |
| la mesa | the table |
| los niños | the children |
| las flores | the flowers |
| Vengo del mercado. | I'm coming from the market. |
| Voy al parque. | I'm going to the park. |
| el agua fría | the cold water |
| las aguas claras | the clear waters |

## Pitfall

Do not skip the contraction: saying *"de el libro"* instead of *"del libro"* is a grammar error. Also, remember that **el** without an accent is an article ("the"), while **él** with an accent is a pronoun ("he") — they sound the same but are spelled differently.
