## Explanation

Relative pronouns introduce relative clauses that modify a noun or pronoun (the antecedent).

**que** — the most common relative pronoun; means "that," "which," or "who." Used for both people and things, in subject or direct object position.
- *El libro que leí era bueno.* (The book that I read was good.)
- *La chica que habla es mi hermana.* (The girl who is speaking is my sister.)

**quien / quienes** — refers only to **people**; used after a **preposition** or in a clause set off by commas.
- *La profesora de quien te hablé es brillante.* (The teacher about whom I told you is brilliant.)
- *Mi amigo, quien vive en París, habla francés.* (My friend, who lives in Paris, speaks French.)

**el que / la que / los que / las que** — agrees in gender and number with the antecedent; often used after prepositions or to add clarity.
- *El edificio en el que vivo es antiguo.* (The building in which I live is old.)

**lo que** — neuter; refers to an idea, clause, or unspecified thing (= "what / the thing that").
- *No entiendo lo que dices.* (I don't understand what you're saying.)
- *Lo que más me gusta es la música.* (What I like most is music.)

**cuyo / cuya / cuyos / cuyas** — possessive relative ("whose"); agrees with the noun it precedes.
- *La escritora cuya novela gané es famosa.* (The writer whose novel I won is famous.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| El coche que compré es rojo. | The car that I bought is red. |
| La persona de quien hablo es mi jefe. | The person I'm talking about is my boss. |
| No sé lo que quiero. | I don't know what I want. |
| La ciudad en la que nací es pequeña. | The city in which I was born is small. |
| El alumno cuyo trabajo leí era brillante. | The student whose work I read was brilliant. |

## Pitfall

Do not use *quien* for things — only for people. *"El libro quien leí"* is wrong; it must be *"El libro que leí."* Also, *lo que* (without an antecedent noun) is different from *el que* (which needs an antecedent noun to agree with).
