## Explanation

Most Spanish nouns form their plural by adding **-s** (vowel ending) or **-es** (consonant ending). Several patterns produce spelling or accent changes.

**1. Nouns ending in -z → change z to c, add -es:**
- *pez → peces* (fish)
- *vez → veces* (time/instance)
- *lápiz → lápices* (pencil)
- *voz → voces* (voice)

**2. Accent mark changes:**
- Nouns with a written accent on the last syllable typically drop the accent in the plural: *lección → lecciones*, *camión → camiones*, *francés → franceses*
- Nouns ending in -n or -s that are unstressed in the singular gain an accent in the plural to maintain stress: *joven → jóvenes*, *orden → órdenes*, *examen → exámenes*

**3. Invariable plurals (no change):**
Nouns ending in an **unstressed vowel + s** are the same in singular and plural:
- *el lunes → los lunes* (Monday/Mondays)
- *el virus → los virus*
- *la crisis → las crisis*
- *el paraguas → los paraguas*

**4. Compound nouns:** Generally only the first element (or no element) changes: *el pasaporte → los pasaportes*.

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| pez → peces | fish → fish (pl.) |
| lápiz → lápices | pencil → pencils |
| lección → lecciones | lesson → lessons |
| joven → jóvenes | young person → young people |
| el lunes → los lunes | Monday → Mondays |
| la crisis → las crisis | crisis → crises |
| voz → voces | voice → voices |

## Pitfall

The -z → -ces spelling change is mandatory: writing *"pezs"* or *"vezs"* is always wrong. Similarly, forgetting the accent shift in *joven → jóvenes* or *examen → exámenes* is a very common written error.
