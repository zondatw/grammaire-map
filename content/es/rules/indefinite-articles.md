## Explanation

Spanish indefinite articles correspond to English "a/an" (singular) and "some" (plural).

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | un | unos |
| Feminine | una | unas |

**When to omit the indefinite article:**

1. **After *ser* with professions** (when unmodified):
   - *Es médico.* (He is a doctor.) — no article
   - *Es una médico excelente.* (She is an excellent doctor.) — article needed when modified by adjective

2. **After *sin* (without) and *con* (with):**
   - *Salió sin paraguas.* (She left without an umbrella.)
   - *Vino con regalo.* (He came with a gift.)

3. **After *como* meaning "as":**
   - *Trabaja como enfermero.* (He works as a nurse.)

4. **With quantity expressions:** *cien personas*, *mil euros* (not *un cien*, *un mil*)

**Unos/unas** can also mean "about/approximately" with numbers: *Hay unos veinte estudiantes.* (There are about twenty students.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| Tengo un perro. | I have a dog. |
| Quiero una manzana. | I want an apple. |
| Hay unos libros en la mesa. | There are some books on the table. |
| Es profesor. | He is a teacher. (profession, no article) |
| Es un profesor brillante. | He is a brilliant teacher. (modified) |
| Salió sin abrigo. | He left without a coat. |

## Pitfall

English speakers often add an article with professions after *ser*: saying *"Es un médico"* is not wrong, but it adds emphasis (implying "a full-fledged doctor"). Without an adjective, simply say *"Es médico"*. The article is mandatory, however, whenever the profession noun is modified.
